# Design: BallBrawl — the full game (path, account, leaderboard, fights with players)

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-08
Branch: main

Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

BallBrawl is playable, but it's "one match and done": there is no reason to come back tomorrow and no one to beat except the computer. The goal is AdSense ad revenue, and revenue = players × matches. So we need (1) a reason to return and (2) a way for players to bring in friends themselves. Plus two fight tweaks from the author: the balls "get too close" (the homing is sticky), and there should be 2 dashes.

## Demand Evidence

- A schoolkid aged 10–14 who plays Roblox/Brawl Stars played and "asked for more" — on the author's computer, next to the author.
- What's missing: nobody has opened the game on their own, on their own phone, the next day. This is the main unknown fact; The Assignment and the stage 1 metrics test it.

## Status Quo

The target player plays Brawl Stars and Roblox at recess and before bed. They are used to: the trophy road, unlocking fighters along the way, skins, rankings, "beating a friend". BallBrawl beats those habits only in that it opens with a link in a second, with no install.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- Person: a younger schoolkid, 10–14, Brawl Stars/Roblox, plays on a phone, in class chats.
- Narrowest wedge: an "I challenge you" link → the friend goes straight into a fight against your squad → loses → wants to win it back and wants a squad of their own. This is retention and distribution at once, which is why the wedge goes into stage 1.

## Constraints

- **Child audience.** We treat the site as child-directed (10–14, mostly under 13). That means: no email, no password, no free text; all advertising is non-personalized and marked "for children" (child-directed). No way to turn personalization on.
- Halal: no random chests/gacha, all rewards are known in advance; ad category blocking in AdSense (as before).
- No real-time server.
- Stack: plain JS + Canvas, no build step; backend — Supabase (free project).
- **Hosting: Cloudflare Pages** (the free tier allows commercial sites; Vercel Hobby prohibits ads — we don't use it). Check Cloudflare's terms at launch.
- The author is alone, in 11th grade; the Supabase/AdSense/domain accounts are on his side (AdSense — in a parent's name).

## Premises (agreed)

1. Money = players × matches. Every feature either brings in players or brings them back tomorrow.
2. Account = anonymous ID + a nickname made of ready-made words ("Quick Hedgehog 482") + a transfer code. Ads are non-personalized.
3. "Online" = fights against squads of real players (controlled by AI) + challenging a friend by link + a leaderboard. Not real time.
4. The main screen is the TROPHY ROAD: wins give trophies, and at points on the path there are balls, skins, coins.
5. First we fix the fight: weaker homing, 2 dashes.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: A game without a server** — rejected: no leaderboard, no "network", no progress transfer.
- **B: Game + Supabase** — chosen.
- **C: Real-time online** — rejected for now: empty queues with only dozens of players, a permanent server, risk of desync between JS engines. Determinism + a command log already exist — we'll come back to it at hundreds of players online.

## Recommended Approach — B, stages by value

Each stage is a working game, tested on a real phone.

### Stage 1 — Fight + challenge by link (no server) → goes out first to 10 players

- **Fight:** homing 1.2 → ~0.55 rad/s; 2 dashes, cooldown ~2.5 s; balance 35–65% by script. Hit: flash, sound (WebAudio), freeze frame — in rendering only, the simulation is not touched.
- **Challenge without a server:** "Challenge a friend" encodes the squad, seed, nickname and trophies into a `#c=` link. The friend opens it → skips the menu and the tutorial → a "You vs Nick" screen for 1 s → fight. **The friend plays a mirrored squad** (the same 3 balls), so it's fair and there's nothing to unlock. The AI opponent gets a level/HP based on the challenger's trophies (as in a normal fight).
- **Challenge rules:** gives no trophies; +10 coins once per link; counts toward quests; replays are allowed, the first result is shown; no "bring back the ball" and no "try a ball"; no interstitial ad is shown in a new player's first match.
- **Result for both (no server):** after the fight the friend sees the outcome and a "Return challenge" button — the link carries his squad and his result against you. When you open it, you see "Nick won/lost your challenge" and immediately play the reply.
- **Offline:** service worker + manifest — the game opens from scratch without a network after the first visit.
- **Measurement:** Cloudflare Web Analytics (cookieless) — visits per day; plus ask 10 players on day 2 and day 3.

### Stage 2 — Network (Supabase)

**Data and ownership.** Table `profiles`: `id` (the profile's own uuid), `owner` (= auth.uid() of the anonymous sign-in), `nick_ids` (IDs of the nickname words, rendered in the viewer's language), `trophies`, `max_trophies`, `squad`, `avatar`, `save` (jsonb: coins, balls, skins, received path rewards, quests, daily reward streak, achievements, statistics), `last_seen`, `flagged`. **Trophies and max_trophies belong to the server**; the client writes the rest into `save` (cosmetics and coins — we trust the client, a known trade-off). RLS is on; there are no direct INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE — only RPCs bound to auth.uid(). Publicly readable: nickname, trophies, avatar, squad.

**Ranked matches — online only.**
- `start_match()` → the server issues a `match_id` and an opponent (server-side `find_opponent` takes the caller's trophies from the DB, ±100, excludes the caller and the 5 most recent opponents; if nothing suitable is found — an AI squad based on trophies).
- `finish_match(match_id, won, flawless)` — accepted once, only if ≥ 20 s and ≤ 10 min have passed since issue. Trophies: win +8 (+1 with no losses), loss −min(8, 2 + ⌊trophies/150⌋), not below 0. Daily gain limit +200 per profile.
- Win/loss is recorded **after** the decision on "bring back the ball" — `finish_match` is called when the match has really ended.
- A profile with > 95% wins over ≥ 30 matches is marked `flagged` and hidden from the leaderboard until the author reviews it. This limits cheating but does not rule it out; a full re-check of the fight from the command log comes later.
- **Without a network** "Fight" turns into "Training" against the AI: coins and quests count, trophies do not. This way there is no trophy sync conflict.

**Opponent strength from trophies.** AI level = clamp(1 + trophies/40, 1, 30), HP multiplier as now (1 + 0.05·(level−1)), with a ceiling at 1160 trophies. The goal is ~55% wins up to 300 trophies, ~50% above that; the growing loss penalty keeps trophies from inflating endlessly. Verified by script before release. Squads of real players: the same AI strength and HP based on the **challenger's** trophies; the trophies of an absent opponent do not change, and the opponent is not notified.

**`save` sync.** At start and after each match: the client sends `save` with `updated_at`; the server stores the latest one. On a conflict (two devices) the newer `updated_at` wins, but the sets (balls, skins, received rewards) are merged, and for coins the maximum is taken. Trophies always come from the server.

**Leaderboard.** Table `matches` (profile_id, delta, ended_at). Tabs: "All" = current trophies, top 100 among those active in the last 30 days and not `flagged`; "Week" = the sum of positive deltas since Monday 00:00 Almaty time (UTC+5), computed by a view over `matches`, no cron. "You are in Nth place" is shown on each tab, for that tab.

**Transfer code.** 8 characters from an alphabet without look-alike letters (23456789ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ), created by the server, single-use: `redeem_code(code)` rewrites the profile's `owner` to the new device and issues a new code. No more than 5 attempts per hour per sign-in. On its next sync the old device sees "Profile transferred" and starts a new one. On screen: "Don't show the code to anyone — it's the key to your profile".
- **Shown in advance:** after the first ranked win — a "Save your code" window (take a photo of the screen / write it down); always visible in Profile. iOS/Safari clears a site's data after 7 days without a visit — the code is the only way to recover.
- Abandoned profiles remain opponents for fights; they drop off the leaderboard after 30 days without a sign-in; they are deleted after 12 months.

**Challenges in the database.** `challenges` (id, owner, squad, seed, created_at), expiry 14 days, link `?c=<id>` (shorter). `answer_challenge(id, won)` — one recorded answer per friend profile (the first). The challenger sees "My challenges" in Profile and a badge with the number of new answers on the Path screen; "Win it back" creates a return challenge. No more than 20 challenges per day per profile. Offline or challenge unavailable → a message and a normal game; Stage 1 links (`#c=`) keep working.

**Privacy.** In the same release as the first network request: a new privacy.html (what is stored: anonymous ID, word nickname, progress, matches; why; retention periods; no personal data), a "Delete profile" button in Profile (RPC `delete_profile` deletes the profile, matches and challenges), a contact email. Remove from privacy the text about "turning off personalization" — there is none at all.

**Ads (child mode).** In all requests — non-personalized ads and a child-directed flag (the exact name of the AdSense H5 parameter — check in the documentation). The consent message for Europe does not offer personalization.

**Measurement.** Table `events` (profile_id, type: session_day / match_end / challenge_created, at) — no personal data; from it come the day-2 return rate and matches per session.

**Loading.** The Supabase client loads lazily after the first frame; if it fails to load, the game starts and works in "Training".

### Stage 3 — Meta: path, balls, skins, quests

- **Trophy Road** — the main screen: a vertical road with reward nodes. Progress is by **max_trophies**: each node is claimed once and is not lost when trophies drop. "Best result" = max_trophies.
- **Balls:** the 5 old ones (except Basic) go onto the path (10, 30, 60, 100, 150 trophies) and stay in the shop for coins; the 6 new ones — Magnet, Bomb, Turtle, Lightning, Hedgehog, Ice — are on the path at 200–600 and also in the shop. "Try a ball" for an ad stays.
- **Skins:** appearance only; sources — the path, coins, day 7 of the daily reward.
- **Quests** (3 per day, coins), a 7-day **daily reward**, **achievements**.
- **Menu:** a bottom bar Path · Balls · Quests · Leaders · Profile + a big "Fight". **Spectator mode** — a "Watch a fight" button in the Balls tab, all 12 balls (this is a tool for TikTok videos).
- **Ads in the new flow:** interstitial — after every 2nd match starting from the 4th, when leaving the results screen **after** the trophy/path animation; rewarded — ×2 coins (win), bring back the ball (loss, once per match), try a ball (squad selection).
- **Save migration v1 → v2:** coins, bought balls and the squad are kept; ladder level → starting trophies (level−1)×15. On the first network sign-in the server accepts the local trophies once, at most 400.

### Stage 4 — Graphics and polish
Menu and transition animations, "living" balls, reward effects on the path, an improved arena.

## Success Criteria

- In the first week players sent ≥ 3 challenge links.
- ≥ 5 matches per session on average (from stage 2 — by `events`).
- All tests green, balance 35–65%, 60 FPS on a mid-range phone, the game opens without a network.

## Distribution Plan

## What I noticed about how you think

- You said: "a person just presses once and that's it". That's not a feature request, it's a diagnosis of the game's main problem, and it's the right one.

### R2-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Stage 1 is now the build that ships to the first 10 players, and its challenge rules assume interstitial ads are running. But the concrete child-directed / non-personalized ad step ("Ads (child mode)") is scheduled for Stage 2, and so is removing the "Ad personalization can be turned off" text from privacy.html. Stage 1 also adds Cloudflare Web Analytics, which the current privacy.html doesn't disclose. If AdSense goes live during Stage 1, children are served ads without the child-directed treatment the Constraints require, next to a privacy page that contradicts the Constraints.

**Remedy**

> Move the child-directed / non-personalized ad step and the privacy.html ad-text fix into Stage 1, and disclose the cookieless analytics there. Alternatively, state that Stage 1 serves no real ads (zero publisher ID or test mode) until the Stage 2 step ships.

### R2-2 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The stage reorder left trophies without a consistent start. Stage 1's link carries "trophies", and the AI is set "by the challenger's trophies (as in a normal fight)", but Stage 1 still runs the 30-level ladder. Trophies first appear with Stage 2's start_match/finish_match. The v1→v2 migration that converts level to trophies is listed in Stage 3, after Stage 2 has already made trophies server-owned and used them for matchmaking. Its one-time import of "local trophies" at the first network login also has no source in this order: Stage 1 has no trophies, and Stage 2 offline training grants none. As written, Stage 2 either starts every player at 0 trophies regardless of level, or depends on a Stage 3 migration.

**Remedy**

> Name the stage where trophies replace the ladder. Move the level → (level−1)×15 conversion and the capped one-time server import into that stage, for example on the first Stage 2 sign-in. Define what the Stage 1 link carries for difficulty: the challenger's level, or trophies derived from it.

### R2-3 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Stage 2 places its user-facing pieces on screens that are only built in Stage 3. "My challenges", the always-visible transfer code and "Delete profile" live in Profile. The answers badge sits on the Path screen, and the leaderboards need the Leaders tab. The bottom bar (Path · Balls · Quests · Leaders · Profile) and the Path itself arrive in Stage 3. The privacy rule requires the delete button in the same release as the first network call, which is Stage 2, so as staged that button has no home.

**Remedy**

> Move a minimal Profile screen and a minimal Leaders screen, with entry points to both, into Stage 2, or define interim entry points in the current menu. Put the answers badge on whatever the Stage 2 main screen is.

### R2-4 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Stage 1's `#c=` link carries "nickname", but word nicknames (Premise 2, `nick_ids`) are only defined in Stage 2, so Stage 1 has no nickname source. The link is also built on the client and stays valid after Stage 2, and its nickname is shown to the friend as "You vs Nick" and "Nick won/lost your challenge". Anyone can edit the fragment and make a classmate's game display arbitrary text, insults included. That is a free-text path in a game the doc treats as child-directed, which contradicts "no free text".

**Remedy**

> Build the word-nickname generator in Stage 1. In links, encode the nickname as word IDs plus its number, render it only from the reviewed word lists, and reject unknown IDs. Validate the other link fields too: known ball IDs, a sane trophy or level range, and the seed format. Fall back to a normal game on any invalid link.

### R2-5 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Ranked trophies change only through finish_match, but the doc never says what happens to a match that is started and never finished. A child who closes or reloads the tab while losing never sends finish_match, so the loss is never deducted. The same happens when a phone loses its network mid-fight. The >95% flag counts only finished matches, so quitting every losing match is a risk-free climb. The doc also leaves open whether a finish_match that fails on a weak connection is retried, which means a real win can be lost, and what happens when a real match ends in under 20 s.

**Remedy**

> Define the match lifecycle. An unfinished match settles as a loss when its 10-minute window expires or when the same profile calls start_match again, settled lazily with no cron. The client retries finish_match until the window closes. Abandoned matches count toward the flag ratio. The client waits until 20 s have passed before reporting, or the minimum is set below the shortest real match.

### R2-6 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Persisting R1-2 obligation. The privacy rewrite and the delete button are now planned, but neither covers all the stored data. delete_profile removes "the profile, matches and challenges", but not the `events` rows keyed by profile_id, or the answers the child recorded on friends' challenges through answer_challenge. Retention is defined for profiles (12 months) and challenges (14 days), but not for `matches` or `events`, although privacy.html must state "retention periods". Nothing says what performs the timed deletions, and the doc otherwise avoids cron.

**Remedy**

> Make delete_profile remove, or cascade to, every row keyed to the profile: events, plus challenge answers both given and received. State the retention period for matches and events. Name the cleanup mechanism, either pg_cron on the free project or deletion performed inside the RPC calls.

### R2-7 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> A new phone, or a phone whose data was wiped (the iOS 7-day case the doc names), signs in anonymously and gets a new profile before the child can enter the code. redeem_code then reassigns the old profile's owner to that same auth.uid(), so one owner now holds two profiles. Every RPC bound to auth.uid() then has no way to tell which profile to act on: start_match, finish_match, save sync and delete_profile. The doc also doesn't say what happens to progress made on the new profile before redeeming.

**Remedy**

> Make owner unique, one profile per auth user. Have redeem_code delete or detach the caller's current profile in the same transaction, and state whether that profile's progress is discarded or merged into the redeemed one.

### R2-8 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Persisting R1-15 obligation. The loss penalty −min(8, 2 + ⌊trophies/150⌋) stops growing at 900 trophies, and AI strength stops at level 30 at 1160 trophies. Above that, the expected change per match is 16·w − 8 plus the flawless bonus. Any player who wins about half the time or more against the fixed level-30 AI therefore gains trophies indefinitely, up to +200 per day. The claim that the growing penalty prevents endless inflation is false at the top. The "All" board would rank top players by play volume rather than skill, which is the original cap → inflation concern.

**Remedy**

> Choose one: keep difficulty rising past 1160, for example by letting the HP multiplier or AI aim keep scaling with trophies; make a loss cost more than a win above the cap; or accept unbounded growth at the top and remove the claim. Verify the chosen rule with the balance script.

### R2-9 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Persisting R1-20 obligation. Stage 1 measurement is now named: Cloudflare Web Analytics plus asking the 10 players on day 2 and day 3. The success criterion "≥ 3 challenge links in the first week" still has no method. The `#c=` fragment never reaches the server, the doc doesn't say whether the cookieless analytics can tell a challenge open from any other visit, and the questions to players cover only next-day returns.

**Remedy**

> Make challenge opens countable in Stage 1. For example, point share links at a distinct path such as /c/ that Cloudflare Pages serves as the game, so the analytics reports it as its own page, and verify that it does. Alternatively, add a question about how many challenges each player sent and received.
